Combined, Arlo Roasters USA shows an overall operating profit of €500,000 − €300,000 = **€200,000**, thin, but not the reported multi-year loss the parent was originally concerned about once the two functions are properly separated. Most of the apparent "loss" pattern in past years actually traces to the e-commerce channel's heavy upfront investment phase, not underpricing in the wholesale business.

## Step 4: Benchmarking the wholesale function

A benchmarking study of independent specialty food and beverage wholesale distributors (screened per Module 3) returns an interquartile range of **2.4% to 4.8%** operating margin, median 3.5%.

The wholesale channel's 3.6% margin falls comfortably inside this range. No adjustment needed on the routine distribution function, it's arm's length.

## Step 5: Diagnosing the e-commerce channel

The e-commerce channel's −4.0% margin can't be benchmarked the same way, there's no meaningful independent comparable for "a subsidiary six years into self-funding a proprietary direct-to-consumer platform and brand presence for its parent's products." This is precisely the Profit Split territory from Lesson 10.1.

An independent valuation of the e-commerce channel's combined economics (once it matures past its investment phase and is assessed across a longer, normalised time horizon) estimates that the channel's ongoing value will split roughly **55% to the US entity** (its self-built platform, customer data, and local audience, a real and separately identifiable contribution) and **45% to Italy** (the underlying Arlo brand and product quality that made the channel viable in the first place). Rather than treating the current-year loss as evidence of underpricing needing a TNMM-style correction, the more defensible conclusion is that Arlo Roasters USA's US-specific digital intangible is a genuine, uncompensated contribution, and the group's broader arrangement (potentially a formal cost-sharing or intangible development arrangement between Italy and the US entity) should be revisited to reflect it.

## The overall conclusion

Two very different outcomes lived inside what looked, at first glance, like one loss-making distributor: a wholesale function that was arm's length all along, and an e-commerce function that wasn't a transfer pricing problem at all, it was an unrecognised, uncompensated intangible contribution. Treating the whole entity as one blended, routine distributor would have led to the wrong diagnosis and the wrong fix.

## Key terms

- **Split-function analysis:** separating a single legal entity's blended results into distinct functions when they have genuinely different risk and value-creation profiles, before applying any method.
- **Uncompensated intangible contribution:** a situation where a subsidiary has built real, valuable intangible assets on its own that existing intercompany arrangements don't yet properly reward.
