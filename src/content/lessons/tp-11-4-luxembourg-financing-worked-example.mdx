## The scenario

Meridian Group establishes a new Luxembourg entity, Meridian Capital S.à r.l., to centralise financing for the group's European distribution subsidiaries. Meridian Capital raises €50,000,000 through a mix of a loan from Meridian AG (€35,000,000) and third-party bank debt guaranteed by Meridian AG (€15,000,000), then on-lends the combined €50,000,000 across Meridian's distribution subsidiaries in France, the Nordics, and elsewhere.

Meridian Capital has a small team (two treasury professionals) who monitor each borrowing subsidiary's basic financial health using a standard internal scorecard, and who have limited authority to adjust lending terms slightly (up to 0.5 percentage points) based on that scorecard, but must escalate anything beyond that to German headquarters for approval, and have no authority to decline a German-approved lending instruction outright.

## Step 1: Characterisation

Applying Module 1's FAR framework: Meridian Capital performs more than a bare pass-through function (it monitors borrower health and has limited independent discretion within a defined band), but far less than a genuine risk-bearing intra-group bank (its real discretion is capped, and major decisions and risk ultimately rest with German headquarters, which also stands behind the third-party bank guarantee). This sits between Lesson 11.2's two categories, closer to the routine intermediary end, but with a genuine, if limited, monitoring function worth recognising.

## Step 2: Method and approach

Given the limited-but-real monitoring function, a pure 2% routine benchmark likely understates Meridian Capital's role, but treating it as a full risk-bearing intra-group bank would overstate it. The most defensible approach: price the base intermediation function using the routine benchmark logic from Lesson 11.2 as a floor, then support a modest additional margin reflecting the genuine (if limited) monitoring and scorecard function, benchmarked where possible against independent loan servicing or credit monitoring fee structures, similar in spirit to a Cost Plus approach (Module 2) for that specific incremental function.

## Key terms

- **Base intermediation return:** the floor-level remuneration for a routine financing pass-through function, referencing Lesson 11.2's 2% guidance.
- **Incremental function pricing:** pricing a genuine, if limited, additional function (like monitoring) separately from the base routine return, rather than ignoring it or overstating it.
