## Why not just average the peer set?

A simple average is sensitive to outliers. One unusually high- or low-margin company in a peer set of ten can drag the average toward a number that doesn't represent the "typical" independent result at all. The interquartile range solves this by discarding the extreme quarters at each end and focusing on the middle 50% of results, where the bulk of genuinely comparable companies tend to sit.

To calculate it: rank the peer set's results (say, operating margins) from lowest to highest, then find:

- **Q1 (first quartile):** the value below which 25% of the results fall
- **Median:** the middle value
- **Q3 (third quartile):** the value below which 75% of the results fall

The range between Q1 and Q3 is the interquartile range, generally accepted under OECD guidance as an appropriate arm's length range when comparability is imperfect (which is almost always).

## Worked example

Meridian France's benchmarking study for its distribution function returns eight comparable companies with these operating margins: 2.1%, 2.8%, 3.4%, 3.9%, 4.3%, 4.6%, 5.5%, 7.2%.

<CalcSteps
  title="Ranking them, then finding the quartiles"
  steps={[
    { expr: 'Median = (3.9 + 4.3) / 2', result: '4.1%' },
    { expr: 'Q1 = median of the lower half (2.8 + 3.4)/2', result: '3.1%' },
    { expr: 'Q3 = median of the upper half (4.6 + 5.5)/2', result: '5.05%' },
  ]}
/>

The interquartile range is **3.1% to 5.05%**. If Meridian France's actual operating margin for the year is 4.4%, it falls comfortably inside the range and is considered arm's length:

<IQRChart
  data={[2.1, 2.8, 3.4, 3.9, 4.3, 4.6, 5.5, 7.2]}
  tested={4.4}

  caption="Each dot is a comparable company. The shaded band is the arm's length range (Q1 to Q3); Meridian France's 4.4% sits inside it."
/>

If it were, say, 1.8%, it would fall below Q1, and Meridian would likely face a transfer pricing adjustment, typically pushing the result up to the median (4.1%) as the standard corrective benchmark many tax authorities apply.

## Try it yourself

A peer set for Meridian's Polish factory returns six comparable cost-plus markups: 4%, 5%, 6%, 8%, 9%, 11%.

1. Calculate the median.
2. Calculate Q1 and Q3.
3. If the Polish factory's actual markup this year was 3%, is it inside or outside the interquartile range, and what would you expect to happen next?

<CollapsibleAnswer>
1. Median = (6+8)/2 = 7%. 2. Q1 = median of (4,5,6) = 5%. Q3 = median of (8,9,11) = 9%. IQR = 5% to 9%. 3. 3% is below Q1 (5%), so it falls outside the range. The factory would likely face a transfer pricing adjustment, typically corrected up to the median (7%) unless a specific, well-documented reason justifies the lower margin.
</CollapsibleAnswer>

## Key terms

- **Interquartile range (IQR):** the middle 50% of a ranked peer set's results, from the first quartile (Q1) to the third quartile (Q3), used as the accepted arm's length range.
- **Quartile:** one of the three points that divide a ranked data set into four equal parts.
