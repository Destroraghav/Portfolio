## A small country with an outsized role

Luxembourg's economy is built substantially around financial services and its position as a jurisdiction of choice for holding companies, financing vehicles, and investment fund structures used by multinational groups headquartered elsewhere. It's common for a large multinational group to have a Luxembourg entity somewhere in its structure, sometimes holding shares in operating subsidiaries, sometimes providing intra-group financing, sometimes licensing intangibles.

This course has already touched Luxembourg twice. Lesson 1.1 opened with the **McDonald's case**, a 2015–2018 European Commission investigation into a Luxembourg tax ruling on franchise royalties, which the Commission ultimately concluded did not amount to illegal state aid, the non-taxation stemmed from a genuine mismatch between the Luxembourg–US tax treaty and US domestic law, not a special favour. Lesson 6.1 covered the **Amazon case**, where a Luxembourg subsidiary was the counterparty to a cost-sharing arrangement that ended up in a multi-year US Tax Court dispute over the value of the intangibles being transferred to it.

Neither case found Luxembourg's own tax administration to have done anything improper. Both cases show the same underlying pattern: because so much genuine cross-border group financing and IP-holding activity is structured through Luxembourg entities, the country sits at the centre of an outsized share of the world's highest-profile transfer pricing questions, which is exactly why understanding its specific rules matters more here than for most jurisdictions.

## What kinds of Luxembourg entities show up most often

| Entity type | Role | TP angle |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Holding company | Owns shares in operating subsidiaries elsewhere, often with limited operational activity of its own | Substance and the arm's length nature of any services it charges |
| Financing company | Borrows from and lends to other group entities, sometimes an internal treasury hub | Module 7's cash pooling and loan pricing apply directly (and Lesson 11.2's financing guidance) |
| IP holding / licensing | Holds or licenses intangibles in some structures | Post-BEPS DEMPE (Module 6): booking IP here without real substance carries real risk |

## Key terms

- **Holding company:** an entity whose primary role is owning shares in other group companies, often with limited independent operational activity.
- **Financing company:** a group entity that provides intra-group loans, guarantees, or treasury functions.
