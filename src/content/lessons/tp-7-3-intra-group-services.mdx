## The benefit test

Before any intra-group service fee is charged, the question is simple to ask and often hard to answer honestly: would an independent company, in the subsidiary's position, be willing to pay an outside provider for this, or would it have performed the activity itself anyway? If the answer is no to both, there's no real service being provided, and no charge is justified, regardless of how the invoice is labelled.

A few categories consistently fail the benefit test:

| Category | Why it fails | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Shareholder activities | Incurred because the parent is a shareholder, not to serve the subsidiary | Preparing consolidated group accounts, the parent's own board meetings |
| Duplicative services | The subsidiary already performs the function competently itself | A second, redundant legal review over an existing local legal team |
| Incidental / passive association | A passive benefit nobody actively provided | A reputational lift from belonging to a well-known group |

## Low value-adding services

For genuine, routine support services, common examples: payroll processing, basic IT support, standard HR administration, the OECD offers a simplified approach: these can be charged out at cost plus a modest markup (commonly around 5%), without requiring an extensive, case-by-case benchmarking study, as long as the services genuinely qualify as low value-adding (not part of the group's core value-creating business, not requiring unique or valuable intangibles, and not generating a significant risk).

## Worked example

Meridian's German headquarters runs a shared payroll processing centre for the whole group, a routine administrative function with clear, low-risk, replicable characteristics, allocated fairly across the subsidiaries based on employee headcount.

Separately, Meridian's German CFO spends time each quarter preparing the consolidated financial statements required for the parent's own public listing. That's a **shareholder activity**, done because Meridian AG itself must comply with its own reporting obligations, not because any subsidiary needs it, and it should not be charged out to the subsidiaries at all.

## Key terms

- **Benefit test:** the requirement that an intra-group service must provide real economic or commercial value to the recipient to justify a charge.
- **Shareholder activity:** a cost incurred purely because of an entity's role as shareholder or parent, not chargeable to subsidiaries.
- **Low value-adding services:** routine, low-risk support services eligible for a simplified cost-plus-modest-markup pricing approach.
