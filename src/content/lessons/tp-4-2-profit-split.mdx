## Why you can't always pick a "tested party"

Every method so far assumes one side of the transaction is routine enough to benchmark against independent comparables. Profit Split exists for the cases where that assumption fails, both parties are doing something genuinely unique, so there's no obvious "simple" side and no real independent company that looks like either of them. Highly integrated global value chains, joint development of valuable IP, or two entities each holding a distinct, hard-to-replicate intangible, all point toward Profit Split.

There are two common approaches:

- **Contribution analysis:** divide the combined profit based on the relative value of each party's functions, assets, and risks, essentially, how much did each side's contribution matter to the profit generated?
- **Residual analysis:** first pay each party a routine return for its ordinary functions (using TNMM or Cost Plus), then split whatever profit is left over, the "residual," based on each party's contribution of unique, valuable intangibles.

## Worked example

Meridian AG's R&D centre and Meridian's Nordic distributor jointly develop a limited-edition "Meridian Arctic" headphone line: the R&D centre designs a genuinely new cold-weather battery technology (a real technical innovation, not routine engineering), and the Nordic distributor invests heavily in a unique regional marketing campaign and secures exclusive placement with major Nordic retailers, both unusual, valuable contributions beyond their normal roles.

The Arctic line generates a combined operating profit of €2,000,000 across both entities in its first year. Using contribution analysis, an independent valuation estimates the relative value of each side's contribution: the R&D centre's battery innovation is judged to represent 60% of the value driving the product's success (a genuine technical differentiator), and the Nordic distributor's exclusive retail access and campaign represent 40% (a real but comparatively smaller driver, since cold-weather battery life was the primary purchase reason cited in customer research).

## Try it yourself

Meridian's Polish factory and Meridian's German parent jointly develop a proprietary manufacturing process that cuts production defects significantly, a genuine process innovation from the factory's engineering team, layered on top of a battery chemistry improvement from German R&D. Combined profit attributable to the improvement: €900,000. An independent study estimates the factory's process innovation represents 35% of the value, and Germany's chemistry work represents 65%.

## Key terms

- **Profit Split Method:** divides the combined profit from a transaction between related parties based on the relative value of their contributions.
- **Contribution analysis:** splits combined profit based on the relative value of each party's functions, assets, and risks.
- **Residual analysis:** pays each party a routine return first, then splits the remaining profit based on unique intangible contributions.
