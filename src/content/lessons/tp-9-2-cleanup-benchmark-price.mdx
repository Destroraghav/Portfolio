## Step 3: Accounting cleanup, finding the true operating profit

Before testing anything, the numbers need cleaning up, exactly the "non-operational intruders" problem from Module 5 and the idle capacity adjustment from Lesson 5.3.

- **Remove non-operational items:** the €600,000 insurance settlement gain and the €350,000 interest expense are both non-operational, they don't reflect the factory's actual manufacturing performance, and both need to be excluded from operating profit.
- **Separate the subcontracted volume:** strip out the €7,200,000 subcontractor cost and its associated 1% markup revenue (€72,000) from the main manufacturing test, since it's a separate, pass-through function.
- **Apply the idle capacity adjustment (Lesson 5.3):** the factory ran at 78% of normal capacity, so its €9,000,000 of fixed indirect manufacturing costs are being spread over a shrunken output base. Restating fixed costs at normal capacity: €9,000,000 × 0.78 = €7,020,000 is the fixed cost absorbed into the routine cost base at this year's actual volume; the remaining €1,980,000 is the idle capacity cost, excluded from the routine margin test (a demand-side shortfall outside the factory's control, not its operating efficiency).

## Step 4: Benchmarking

A benchmarking study (Module 3's process) of independent contract manufacturers in the consumer electronics sector, screened for independence, functional comparability, and data quality, returns a peer set with these Return on Total Costs figures: 4.5%, 5.8%, 6.4%, 7.1%, 7.9%, 8.6%, 9.8%, 11.2%.

## Step 5: Diagnosis and conclusion

The factory's capacity- and cost-cleaned Return on Total Costs is 9.2%, landing exactly at the top edge (Q3) of the 6.1%–9.2% interquartile range. This is a clean result: the Polish factory's manufacturing profitability for the year is arm's length, right at the upper bound but still inside the accepted range, **no adjustment required** for the core manufacturing function.

Separately, the subcontracted volume: €7,200,000 in subcontractor invoices, remunerated at the agreed 1% markup, generating €72,000 in additional revenue for administering the arrangement. This pass-through fee should be benchmarked on its own terms (typically against market rates for similar administrative pass-through or agency arrangements) rather than folded into the main manufacturing test, consistent with the answer in Lesson 9.1.

## Key terms

- **Accounting cleanup:** removing non-operational items (one-off gains, financial items) and normalising for distortions like idle capacity or pass-through costs, before benchmarking.
- **Return on Total Costs:** the PLI used in this case, operating profit divided by total operating costs.
