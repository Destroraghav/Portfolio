## The situation

Arlo Roasters is an Italian specialty coffee group: an Italian parent that owns the Arlo brand, proprietary roasting recipes, and sets group strategy; a Brazilian subsidiary that sources and does basic processing of green coffee beans; and a US distribution subsidiary, Arlo Roasters USA, that imports roasted coffee and sells it to US retailers and a growing direct-to-consumer e-commerce channel it built and runs itself.

Arlo Roasters USA has posted a net operating loss for the past two years, even as the wider Arlo Group has been solidly profitable, driven by strong European sales. The Italian parent wants to understand whether this is a transfer pricing problem (an underpriced product being sold into the US at too high a transfer price, or a distributor not being compensated fairly) or something that has a legitimate business explanation.

**Additional facts:** Six years ago, Arlo Roasters USA began building its own direct-to-consumer e-commerce platform and a US-specific brand presence, largely independent of the Italian parent's European marketing. This required significant, sustained local investment, its own content, its own influencer partnerships, its own customer data and loyalty programme, none of it directed or funded by Italy. That US-specific digital brand presence has become genuinely valuable: the direct e-commerce channel now drives 35% of US revenue at a much higher margin than the wholesale-to-retailer business, a channel that didn't exist six years ago and that Italy had no real hand in building.

## Step 1: Characterisation (FAR analysis)

At first glance, Arlo Roasters USA looks like a routine importer-distributor: it buys roasted coffee from the group and resells it. But a proper functional analysis (Module 1) needs to look past that first impression.

<FARBlocks
  functions={['Routine wholesale distribution to US retailers', 'Six years of independently-built e-commerce infrastructure, content, and customer relationships']}
  assets={['No ownership of the Arlo brand or roasting recipes (those sit with Italy)', 'Owns its own customer database, loyalty programme, and e-commerce platform, built and funded itself']}
  risks={['Carries its own inventory risk', 'Self-funded years of e-commerce investment with no guarantee it would pay off, a risk Italy did not share']}
/>

This is not a clean, routine limited-risk distributor. It has a genuine claim to a DEMPE-style contribution (Module 6): the US-specific digital brand and customer relationship intangible was developed, enhanced, and is being exploited primarily by the US entity itself, largely independent of the Italian parent's own marketing intangibles.

## Step 2: Why this complicates method selection

If Arlo Roasters USA were a routine distributor, Module 4's diagnostic playbook would apply directly, a sustained loss while the group is profitable is a red flag pointing toward a straightforward TNMM benchmarking exercise and likely correction. But the self-built e-commerce intangible changes the picture: part of the US margin story may reflect a real, valuable contribution the US entity made on its own, not merely an underpriced transfer price from Italy.

This points toward a **hybrid approach**: benchmark the routine wholesale distribution function using TNMM (there's no dispute that reselling roasted coffee to retailers is a fairly routine activity), but treat the e-commerce/direct-to-consumer channel as a separate question, potentially requiring a Profit Split-style analysis (Module 4, Lesson 4.2) if the US intangible's contribution can be reliably valued, rather than assuming the whole entity should be judged on a single blended distributor margin.

## Key terms

- **Loss-making red flag:** a persistent operating loss at an entity that's supposedly routine, while the wider group is profitable, generally requires investigation before assuming it's acceptable.
- **Self-built intangible:** an intangible asset a subsidiary develops and funds on its own initiative, potentially entitling it to compensation beyond a routine functional margin (Module 6's DEMPE framework).
