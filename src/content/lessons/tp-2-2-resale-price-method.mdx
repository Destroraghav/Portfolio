## Why RPM exists

CUP needs a near-identical product transaction to compare against, which is often impossible to find once a product carries any real branding or customisation. RPM sidesteps that problem for distribution businesses by not comparing the product's price directly at all. Instead, it compares the margin a distributor earns, since gross distribution margins for a given type of business (electronics, apparel, groceries) tend to cluster in a predictable range regardless of exactly which product is being resold.

This makes RPM well suited to distributors that add limited value: they buy finished goods and resell them essentially as-is, without meaningfully transforming, rebranding, or engineering the product further. The less a distributor changes the product, the more reliable RPM tends to be, because the margin comparison isn't distorted by extra functions the benchmark companies don't perform.

<FormulaCard label="Resale Price Method">Transfer Price = Resale Price × (1 − Gross Margin%)</FormulaCard>

The gross margin percentage comes from what comparable independent distributors earn in the same type of business.

## Worked example

Meridian France buys finished headphones from Meridian's Polish factory and resells them to French electronics retailers for €90 per unit (the resale price to an independent customer). A benchmarking study of independent electronics distributors performing a similar function finds they typically earn a gross margin of 35% on resale price.

<CalcSteps
  title="Applying RPM"
  steps={[
    { expr: 'Transfer Price = €90 × (1 − 0.35)', result: '€58.50' },
  ]}
/>

If Meridian's actual intercompany purchase price is €70, that leaves Meridian France with only a much thinner margin:

<CalcSteps
  steps={[
    { expr: 'Actual margin = (90 − 70) / 90', result: '22%', note: 'below the 35% benchmark' },
  ]}
/>

That's a signal worth investigating: either Meridian France is overpaying its Polish factory for the goods (shifting profit toward Poland), or there's a real functional difference that justifies the lower margin, and that difference needs to be identified and explained.

## Try it yourself

Meridian's Nordic distributor buys headphones from the Polish factory and resells them to Norwegian retailers for kr 950 per unit. Comparable independent Nordic electronics distributors earn a gross margin of 30%.

1. Calculate the arm's length transfer price using RPM.
2. If Meridian's actual intercompany price is kr 640, is the Nordic distributor's margin above or below the benchmark, and what would that suggest?

<CollapsibleAnswer>
1. 950 × (1 − 0.30) = kr 665. 2. Actual margin = (950 − 640)/950 = 32.6%, which is above the 30% benchmark. That's not automatically a problem (being within or near the range is generally fine), but a margin noticeably above the top of an independent range would prompt a closer look at whether the distributor is earning more than its functions justify.
</CollapsibleAnswer>

## Key terms

- **Resale price method (RPM):** prices a controlled purchase by working backward from the resale price to an independent customer, minus an arm's length gross margin.
- **Gross margin:** resale price minus cost of goods sold, expressed as a percentage of resale price.
